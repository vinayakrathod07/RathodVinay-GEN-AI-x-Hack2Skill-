Emergency Scam Recovery Action Plan

If you already deposited a check or sent money

1. Contact Your Bank's Fraud Department Immediately

Call the number on the back of your debit/credit card right away. Tell them: "I received a fraudulent check from a recruitment scam and need to report it before it bounces." If you wired funds or sent Zelle/CashApp, request an immediate transaction recall.

2. Stop All Contact with the Fraudster

Do not alert or confront the scammer. Cease replying to messages on WhatsApp, Telegram, or email. Save screenshots of all chat conversations, sent checks, and transaction IDs as evidence.

3. Freeze Your Credit If SSN/ID Was Shared

Place a free credit freeze or fraud alert with the three major bureaus to prevent loans or credit cards being opened in your name:

Equifax Experian TransUnion
4. File Official Law Enforcement Reports

Submitting official reports establishes a legal paper trail that banks often require to reverse fraudulent charges:

FTC ReportFraud.ftc.gov ↗ FBI IC3.gov ↗

Recruitment & Job Offer Scam Playbook

Learn how criminals structure fake job offers

💸 Scheme 1: Fake Equipment Check / Advance-Fee Voucher

The "employer" emails an e-check (e.g. $3,500) to buy a MacBook and home office setup from their "certified vendor". Banks are legally required to make funds visible in 1-2 days, but authentic clearing takes up to 2 weeks. Once you wire the money to their vendor, the check bounces and you owe the bank $3,500.

💬 Scheme 2: Text-Only Interview on Telegram/WhatsApp

Legitimate companies never conduct an entire hiring interview via Telegram, WhatsApp, or Signal text questionnaires without video calls or verified enterprise email. Text-only interviews indicate an automated criminal gang.

🪪 Scheme 3: Early Identity & Banking Harvesting

Before you sign a verified contract or meet the team, they demand copies of your driver's license, passport, SSN, and voided bank checks. These are immediately sold on darknet forums or used to open fraudulent credit lines.

🌐 Scheme 4: Lookalike Domains (Typosquatting)

Scammers register domains like apple-careers.biz or stripe-recruitment.online. Always verify that emails come from the exact corporate domain (e.g., @apple.com, @stripe.com).
